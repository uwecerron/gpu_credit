\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Several synthetic-dollar protocols now take stablecoin deposits on-chain and lend them to companies that operate GPU clusters, paying depositors from the interest. USD.AI is one of the larger ones. It has a \$100\,M credit facility from Bullish~\cite{bullish2025}, and its tokens have been proposed as collateral on Aave~\cite{aavearfc}. Futures on GPU rental prices are also being built so lenders can hedge the same risk~\cite{architect2026}. Lenders, risk curators and auditors are pricing GPU-backed credit, but we are not aware of a published independent account of what the chain shows about it.

The GPUs, the borrowers' revenues and the loan agreements are off-chain. The recorded liabilities are not. Deposits, loan disbursements, repayments, fees, redemption requests and bridge transfers leave event logs on a public chain. Those logs cannot prove that a GPU exists. They can reconstruct recorded loan terms and payments, yield transfers, token custody and completed redemption times. They cannot establish beneficial ownership, collateral perfection or ultimate borrower cash flow. A depositor or a risk curator needs both kinds of evidence before relying on the off-chain story.

We rebuild USD.AI from raw logs using the Covalent GoldRush API~\cite{goldrush} and the protocol's source code~\cite{usdaisrc}, and ask seven questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[RQ1] Where does deposited money go?
\item[RQ2] Where does the yield come from, and is NAV growth backed by cash from borrowers?
\item[RQ3] Who holds the yield-bearing token?
\item[RQ4] How concentrated are the borrowers?
\item[RQ5] How do redemptions behave, and do they follow GPU rental prices?
\item[RQ6] Which chains does the protocol live on?
\item[RQ7] How do loan originations compare, descriptively, with payments on Akash and Livepeer, two distinct on-chain compute venues?
\end{enumerate}
We also check every headline number against the vault's own accounting at the same block at which the event study ends, and we trace one kind of vault outflow, escrow pre-funding, through the wallets it passed.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item An open, tested pipeline that decodes every event from source-code signatures, follows loans through a V1 to V2 migration and through refinancings, and reproduces all results offline from a hash-verified dataset.
\item A reconstruction of a GPU-backed loan book from chain data: \NLoans{} loans to \NBorrowers{} borrower addresses, \Originated{} originated and \Outstanding{} outstanding.
\item A cash-coverage test that separates yield received in cash from yield credited through NAV, and a sensitivity that separates borrower cash from reserve yield, issuer incentives and one-off transfers.
\item A reconstruction of the collateral from the protocol's hardware-token metadata: model, unit count, stated value and loan-to-value for every active loan.
\item Measurements of holder concentration, the redemption queue and supply across five chains.
\item A same-block reconciliation showing that \DTShare{} of reported vault assets is an escrow claim on funds that were redeemed to PYUSD and sent onward before any matching loan existed.
\item A descriptive comparison of GPU credit originations with spending on two decentralized compute venues over the same period; these are different economic quantities, not an estimate of total compute demand.
\item Monitoring indicators that lenders and curators can run with the same code.
\end{enumerate}

Earlier empirical work on DeFi lending studied interest rates and liquidations in over-collateralized markets with on-chain collateral~\cite{gudgeon2020defi,qin2021liquidations,werner2022sok}. Stablecoin research models peg stability and run risk~\cite{klagesmundt2020stablecoins,gorton2023wildcat,diamond1983runs}. A GPU-backed dollar fits neither. Its collateral is off-chain and illiquid, its assets are term loans, and holders exit through a queue that an administrator services. Our earlier paper modeled the economic life of GPUs as assets~\cite{cerron2026gpu}. This one looks at the debt financed against them.
