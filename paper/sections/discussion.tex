\section{Discussion}\label{sec:disc}

\subsection{What the chain can and cannot tell a lender}
Chain data is strong on recorded liabilities and token movements. It permits a reconstructed loan book, borrower-address ledger, repayment history and redemption queue, with an exact-block comparison to selected vault accounting views. It is weak on physical assets and contractual rights. It cannot establish that a GPU exists, that it is generating revenue, or that a lender can repossess it. Three findings sit at that boundary. First, borrower interest and fees contributed \KappaBorrowers{} of NAV-accrued yield over this window. Stablecoin incentives and two extraordinary transfers account for much of the remainder, so the historical \CashCoverage{} cash-coverage ratio cannot be interpreted as recurring borrower cash generation. Second, \DTShare{} of reported assets was attributed to pre-funded escrow without a matching on-chain loan or wrapped collateral at the snapshot; the off-chain contractual claim is unknown. Third, loan maturities extend \YearsToMaturity{} more years on average while holders can request redemption through a periodically serviced queue. The snapshot idle balance provides near-term liquidity, but a severe run would require further funding, scheduled repayments or asset sales. The headline NAV alone does not resolve these risks.

\subsection{What a neocloud unwind would do}\label{sec:unwind}
The borrowers are GPU operators, some of which participate in the broader neocloud sector. Published commentary estimates more than \$20\,B of GPU-backed debt across that sector~\cite{friedman2025}, but this is context, not a verified denominator for the present loan book. H100 posted rental prices declined substantially from early 2024 to late 2025 in the cited sources~\cite{friedman2025}, and the GetDeploying sample used here has a centralized median of \CentralH{}. Posted H100 rates do not reveal the realized contracted revenue of B200- and B300-backed borrowers. Hardware-life assumptions also vary with workload, resale markets and maintenance~\cite{cerron2026gpu}.

USD.AI provides an observable subset of GPU-backed credit. Table~\ref{tab:stress} applies hypothetical recoveries to outstanding loan claims at block \StateBlock{}, not to the independently appraised resale value of collateral. It is arithmetic, not a credit model. It omits recovery delays, enforcement costs and correlated equipment-price declines. The protocol's documentation also describes possible residual-value insurance, but this study has not verified any policy's coverage, exclusions, claimant rights or insurer solvency~\cite{usdaidocs}; the table therefore assumes no incremental insurance recovery. The largest borrower address holds \TopOneOut{} of outstanding principal and the three largest \TopThreeOut{}. At a 40\% recovery of the debt claim, a default by the largest costs \StressOnePct{} of assets and leaves modeled NAV at \StressOneNAV{}; the three largest cost \StressThreePct{} and leave NAV at \StressThreeNAV{}. If every borrower defaults at the same assumed recovery, NAV falls to \StressAllNAV{}. If all open escrow value is separately written off, modeled NAV is \StressWorstNAV{}; that is a deliberately severe combined scenario, not an estimate of its probability.

Three features condition how a loss might propagate. First, the vault prices deposits using optimistic NAV and redemptions using conservative NAV. The documented default and recovery process can involve delayed recognition of actual proceeds, so the sequence between missed payments, formal default, recovery and redemptions matters~\cite{usdaidocs}. Second, exits are queued. At the snapshot, idle deposit balance covers the pending queue \IdleOverPending{} times over, but this is a point-in-time ratio, not a stressed liquidity guarantee. Third, sUSDai is held in other DeFi contracts: \EthMorpho{} of Ethereum supply is at Morpho Blue and \PendleArb{} of Arbitrum supply at Pendle. Depending on collateral parameters, leverage and market depth, a NAV impairment could induce liquidations or discounts; the present data does not establish their magnitude.

Related GPU-financing structures may share sensitivity to equipment resale prices and customer demand~\cite{gaib2025}, although exposure varies by borrower contracts, collateral, security rights and funding terms. Three on-chain questions remain useful: how much realized vault income comes from borrower payments rather than reserve yield; how concentrated the current loan exposures are; and how queued redemptions compare with liquid assets. These indicators can be monitored from the reconstructed ledger, while their interpretation requires off-chain contract and operating evidence.

\subsection{Risks the chain shows only in outline}
Seven risks are visible on-chain but cannot be sized from it.

\emph{Related parties.} Some submitting accounts recur across the protocol multisig and escrow-facility transactions. The largest borrower address, fee recipient and escrow-related wallets also transact with a common high-activity destination. This supports a question about control and economic affiliation, not a conclusion of common ownership or impropriety. A lender cannot treat \NBorrowers{} borrower addresses as \NBorrowers{} legally independent credit exposures without an entity map.

\emph{Key compromise.} Administrator permissions and Safe signing arrangements create operational dependencies. Their actual security depends on signer custody, quorum, delay mechanisms and incident response, none of which is established by counting on-chain transactions. We have not tested the operators' private-key controls.

\emph{Fixed loans, floating benchmark.} The loans are fixed at \RateMin{} to \RateMax{} for \YearsToMaturity{} more years. The yield holders compare against moves: Treasury bills, and the PYUSD and M\textsuperscript{0} incentives that supplied most of the cash yield so far. If those fall, the vault's spread widens; if they rise, the vault holds below-market paper and the queue fills.

\emph{Custody, title and valuation.} The hardware is described through token metadata down to server models and, for some early loans, serial numbers (Section~\ref{sec:collateral}). What is not independently established is the unit's existence, location, perfected security interest, liquidation priority, insurance coverage, or whether stated values are appraisals or purchase prices. Book LTV of \BookLTV{} is therefore a ratio of contractual principal to protocol-stated collateral value, not a verified liquidation LTV.

\emph{Bridged exits.} \BridgedShare{} of shares live on Ethereum, Base, Plasma and Solana. A holder there must bridge back to Arbitrum before joining the queue. A paused bridge pauses the exit for two fifths of the supply.

\emph{Leverage on top.} Pendle holds \PendleArb{} of Arbitrum sUSDai and Morpho Blue \EthMorpho{} of Ethereum sUSDai. Fixed-yield buyers in Pendle have locked in today's rate; a NAV mark hits them first, and the leveraged positions sit there.

\emph{Classification.} This is pooled private credit with a yield-bearing token. Payment-stablecoin rules in the United States now distinguish tokens that pay yield from those that do not, and the treatment of a structure like this one is not settled. A ruling could change who may hold it or how it may be marketed.

\subsection{What this means for different readers}
\emph{Traders.} Three observable indicators deserve monitoring: delays in scheduled repayments (currently the longest observed gap is \MaxDaysSincePayment{} days), the queued-redemption to idle-cash ratio (currently \IdleOverPending{}$\times$ covered), and sUSDai's market discount to its accounting NAV. The repository computes these indicators; their ability to predict losses or market discounts has not yet been evaluated out of sample. The conservative redemption price (\RedemptionPrice{}) and deposit price (\DepositPrice{}) also differ because of the vault's valuation conventions.

\emph{Institutional allocators.} Review the two open escrows, which represent \DTShare{} of reported assets but have no matching originated loan at the snapshot. Verify legal claims, beneficiaries, expiry provisions and any collateral or insurance independently. The borrower-interest-and-fee ratio (\KappaBorrowers{}) is a historical contribution to NAV growth, not a stand-alone sustainable APY or debt-service coverage measure. Underwrite the \TopOneOut{} single-name share of current principal, reconcile protocol-stated \BookLTV{} with appraised liquidation values, and assess insured and uninsured recovery paths separately (Table~\ref{tab:stress}).

\emph{Neoclouds.} Public event logs can lower the cost of verifying payment and ownership records, although this study does not compare audit costs with bank credit. Borrowers could make the asset side easier to underwrite by providing independently attestable utilization, contractual revenues, power expense, location, title and recovery rights. Whether such disclosure would reduce borrowing costs relative to the observed \RateWavg{} weighted-average coupon remains an empirical question.

\emph{Researchers.} The included frozen extracts match their SHA-256 manifest and support the reported reconciliations. The small data handover does not contain the complete Livepeer extract or full analysis pipeline, so full offline reproduction additionally requires those materials. The method may transfer to other GPU-credit protocols with equivalent event and state coverage. Open questions include independent collateral valuations, borrower revenue, and recoveries once a loan defaults. A second protocol and a longer window would make this a comparative study.

\subsection{Monitoring indicators}
The pipeline can run on a schedule. We suggest six indicators, each computed by code in the repository:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Cash coverage} $\kappa$ by month, decomposed into borrower receipts, recurring reserve income, and exceptional transfers.
\item \emph{Pre-funded share}: \texttt{depositTimelockBalance} over \texttt{totalAssets}, with the age of each open escrow.
\item \emph{Borrower HHI} on outstanding principal, and the largest borrower's share.
\item \emph{Queue pressure}: pending redemptions against idle deposit balance, and the age of the oldest pending request.
\item \emph{Missed payments}: loans whose last repayment is older than their repayment interval.
\item \emph{Peg stress}: the market discount of USDai and sUSDai to NAV.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Other GPU-backed protocols}
Other protocols also tokenize GPU financing or GPU revenue, and some issue their own synthetic dollars, such as GAIB's AID~\cite{gaib2025}. The same method works for any of them whose contracts run on a chain GoldRush indexes: decode from source, rebuild the loan book, reconcile against state and trace the cash. Comparing them on cash coverage, pre-funding and borrower concentration is a natural next study.

\subsection{Data coverage}
Without a unified API, several steps would have needed an archive node and custom indexing. GoldRush provided complete decoded histories for the lending contracts, event scans across 182 block windows, holder lists on four chains, holder lists at past blocks, one call listing every chain an address has used, cross-chain wallet balances and daily token prices. Three gaps remain. GoldRush does not offer \texttt{eth\_call}, so the state check used a public RPC. Holder lists at past blocks are available only on its Foundational chains, so we have them for Ethereum but not Arbitrum. Akash and other Cosmos chains are not indexed.

\subsection{Limitations}
We study one protocol over 17 months. The loan book is reconstructed from selected events, and both API and node checks use those same event definitions. Identical raw logs do not independently prove complete contract coverage, correct borrower identities or the economic meaning of every transaction. The event cutoff and primary state check now share a block, but the \LoanBookGap{} loan-balance residual remains unexplained by an itemized accounting bridge. The reserve observation uses a later block, and the Arbitrum and Ethereum holder-to-supply checks have unequal observation times. Two V1 topic hashes remain unidentified. Hardware-token metadata is mutable and was retrieved after the study cutoff; two host-served records are not minted. The aggregate GPU count includes mixed-unit rows requiring further inspection. Holder-address concentration is not beneficial-owner concentration. Completed redemption waits exclude still-pending requests, and most loans have not matured. The H100 price series is neither model-matched to the B200/B300-heavy collateral nor a record of borrower realized revenue. Livepeer payments mix transcoding and AI inference; Akash figures come from its own API. The current small handover archive omits the large Livepeer raw extract, which must be supplied separately for a fully offline reconstruction. We have not independently verified executed loan documents, title, collateral custody, enforceable insurance, leases, utilization, operating expenses or customer receivables. No off-chain person or entity is attributed to a wallet; the data do not demonstrate misuse of funds.

\subsection{Ethics and right of reply}
All data is public. We do not name individuals or link wallets to companies. We invite corrections and an explanation of the escrow accounting from USD.AI and will incorporate any substantiated response in a revised version.

\section{Conclusion}
Event logs allow a detailed reconstruction of recorded GPU-backed lending and vault flows: \Originated{} in gross loan originations to \NBorrowers{} borrower addresses, historical income from borrower payments and base yield, lumpy redemption servicing, and \DTShare{} of reported assets attributed to pre-funded escrows without matching on-chain loan originations at the snapshot. These are financial-observability findings, not evidence that credit has outrun underlying compute demand. Their significance is the separation between auditable financial records and off-chain collateral, contracted revenue and legal recovery. A credible machine-credit risk measure will require both sets of information. Comparing this framework across protocols and testing whether its indicators forecast repayment, liquidity or recovery outcomes remain tasks for further research.
