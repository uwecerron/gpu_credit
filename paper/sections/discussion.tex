\section{Discussion}\label{sec:disc}

\subsection{What the chain can and cannot tell a lender}
Chain data is strong on recorded liabilities and token movements. It permits a reconstructed loan book, a borrower-address ledger, a repayment history and a redemption queue, with a same-block comparison to the vault's reported accounting. It is weak on physical assets and contractual rights. It cannot establish that a GPU exists, that it is generating revenue, or that a lender can repossess it. Three findings sit at that boundary. First, harvested base yield from stablecoin issuers (M\textsuperscript{0} and PYUSD) exceeded borrower interest over this particular window, although a large one-off wM event limits inference about future yield composition. Second, \DTShare{} of reported assets was attributed to pre-funded escrow without a matching on-chain loan or wrapped collateral at the snapshot; the off-chain contractual claim is unknown. Third, loan maturities extend \YearsToMaturity{} more years on average while holders can request redemption through a periodically serviced queue. The snapshot idle balance provides near-term liquidity, but a severe run would require further funding, scheduled repayments or asset sales. The headline NAV alone does not resolve these risks.

\subsection{What a neocloud unwind would do}\label{sec:unwind}
The borrowers are GPU operators, some of which participate in the broader neocloud sector. Published commentary estimates more than \$20\,B of GPU-backed debt across that sector from lenders such as Blackstone, PIMCO, Carlyle and Macquarie~\cite{friedman2025}, and reports CoreWeave's five-year credit default swaps at distressed levels in October 2026~\cite{gokhshtein2026}; this is context, not a verified denominator for the present loan book. Posted H100 rental prices declined from \$7 to \$10 per hour in early 2024 to \$2 to \$4 by late 2025 in the cited sources~\cite{friedman2025}, and the GetDeploying sample used here has a centralized median of \CentralH{}. Posted H100 rates do not reveal the realized contracted revenue of B200- and B300-backed borrowers. Hardware-life assumptions also vary with workload, resale markets and maintenance~\cite{cerron2026gpu}; the cited debt structures assume five to six years of useful value, while some operators and investors argue for two to three~\cite{friedman2025}.

USD.AI provides an observable subset of GPU-backed credit. Table~\ref{tab:stress} applies hypothetical recoveries to outstanding loan claims at block \StateBlock{}, not to an independently appraised resale value of the collateral. It is arithmetic, not a credit model. It omits recovery delays, enforcement costs and correlated equipment-price declines. The protocol's documentation also describes possible residual-value insurance, but this study has not verified any policy's coverage, exclusions, claimant rights or insurer solvency~\cite{usdaidocs}; the table therefore assumes no incremental insurance recovery. The largest borrower address holds \TopOneOut{} of outstanding principal and the three largest \TopThreeOut{}. At a 40\% recovery of the debt claim, a default by the largest costs \StressOnePct{} of assets and leaves modeled NAV at \StressOneNAV{}; the three largest cost \StressThreePct{} and leave NAV at \StressThreeNAV{}. If every borrower defaults at the same assumed recovery, NAV falls to \StressAllNAV{}. If all open escrow value is separately written off, modeled NAV is \StressWorstNAV{}; that is a deliberately severe combined scenario, not an estimate of its probability.

Three features condition how a loss might propagate. First, the vault prices deposits using an optimistic NAV and redemptions using a conservative one, and nothing in the contracts marks a loan down before a breach event. A borrower that stops paying would show up as a missed monthly repayment, then as a \texttt{LoanBreached} event, and only then in NAV. The documented default and recovery process can involve delayed recognition of actual proceeds, so the sequence between missed payments, formal default, recovery and redemptions matters~\cite{usdaidocs}. Second, exits are queued. At the snapshot, the idle deposit balance covers the pending queue \IdleOverPending{} times over, but this is a point-in-time ratio, not a stressed liquidity guarantee; in a run the idle balance goes first, then the vault waits on \YearsToMaturity{} more years of loan maturities. Third, sUSDai is held in other DeFi contracts: \EthMorpho{} of the Ethereum supply is at Morpho Blue and \PendleArb{} of the Arbitrum supply at Pendle. Depending on collateral parameters, leverage and market depth, a NAV impairment could induce liquidations or discounts; the present data does not establish their magnitude.

Related GPU-financing structures, including protocols that issue their own synthetic dollar~\cite{gaib2025}, may share sensitivity to equipment resale prices and customer demand, although exposure varies by borrower contracts, collateral, security rights and funding terms. Three on-chain questions remain useful across them: how much realized vault income comes from borrower payments rather than reserve yield; how concentrated the current loan exposures are; and how queued redemptions compare with liquid assets. These indicators can be monitored from the reconstructed ledger with the code in this repository, while their interpretation requires off-chain contract and operating evidence.

\subsection{Risks the chain shows only in outline}
Seven risks are visible on-chain but cannot be sized from it.

\emph{Related parties.} Some submitting accounts recur across the protocol multisig and the escrow facility's transactions. The largest borrower address, the fee recipient and the escrow-related wallets also transact with a common high-activity destination. This supports a question about control and economic affiliation, not a conclusion of common ownership or impropriety. A lender cannot treat \NBorrowers{} borrower addresses as \NBorrowers{} legally independent credit exposures without an entity map, and the chain does not supply it.

\emph{Key compromise.} The admin role can pause every core contract, and the escrow and multisig are controlled by a handful of externally owned accounts. Their actual security depends on signer custody, quorum, delay mechanisms and incident response, none of which is established by counting on-chain transactions, and we have not tested the operators' private-key controls. The failure mode is not hypothetical: days before this paper was finished, leaked environment variables at a hosting provider let one attacker drain 231 wallets across 22 chains over about seven hours, because private keys sat on application servers~\cite{railway2026}. The same failure applied to a signer here would not drain the vault directly, but it could pause redemptions or move escrow.

\emph{Fixed loans, floating benchmark.} The loans are fixed at \RateMin{} to \RateMax{} for \YearsToMaturity{} more years. The yield holders compare against moves: Treasury bills, and the PYUSD and M\textsuperscript{0} incentives that supplied most of the cash yield so far. If those fall, the vault's spread widens; if they rise, the vault holds below-market paper and the queue fills.

\emph{Custody, title and valuation.} The hardware is described through token metadata down to server models and, for some early loans, serial numbers (Section~\ref{sec:collateral}). What is not independently established is each unit's existence, location, perfected security interest, liquidation priority and insurance coverage, or whether stated values are appraisals or purchase prices. Book LTV of \BookLTV{} is therefore a ratio of contractual principal to protocol-stated collateral value, not a verified liquidation LTV.

\emph{Bridged exits.} \BridgedShare{} of shares live on Ethereum, Base, Plasma and Solana. A holder there must bridge back to Arbitrum before joining the queue. A paused bridge pauses the exit for two fifths of the supply.

\emph{Leverage on top.} Pendle holds \PendleArb{} of Arbitrum sUSDai and Morpho Blue \EthMorpho{} of Ethereum sUSDai. Fixed-yield buyers in Pendle have locked in today's rate; a NAV mark hits them first, and the leveraged positions sit there.

\emph{Classification.} This is pooled private credit with a yield-bearing token. The GENIUS Act of 2025 bars permitted payment-stablecoin issuers from paying yield to holders, and the treatment of a structure like this one, which is not a payment stablecoin but is marketed alongside them, is not settled. A ruling could change who may hold it or how it may be marketed.

\subsection{What this means for different readers}
\emph{Traders.} Three observable indicators deserve monitoring: delays in scheduled repayments (currently the longest observed gap is \MaxDaysSincePayment{} days), the queued-redemption to idle-cash ratio (currently \IdleOverPending{}$\times$ covered), and sUSDai's market discount to its accounting NAV. The repository computes these indicators; their ability to predict losses or market discounts has not been evaluated out of sample. The conservative redemption price (\RedemptionPrice{}) and deposit price (\DepositPrice{}) differ because of the vault's valuation conventions, and the gap is the protocol's own view of how much accrued interest is at risk.

\emph{Institutional allocators.} Review the two open escrows, which represent \DTShare{} of reported assets but have no matching originated loan at the snapshot, and verify legal claims, beneficiaries, expiry provisions and any collateral or insurance independently. The borrower-interest-and-fee ratio (\KappaBorrowers{}) is a historical contribution to NAV growth, not a stand-alone sustainable APY or a debt-service coverage measure. Underwrite the \TopOneOut{} single-name share of current principal, reconcile the protocol-stated \BookLTV{} with appraised liquidation values, and assess insured and uninsured recovery paths separately (Table~\ref{tab:stress}). Ask who appraised the stated collateral values and what the fleet's utilization is; the chain gives the model and count but not the revenue.

\emph{Neoclouds.} Public event logs can lower the cost of verifying payment and ownership records, although this study does not compare audit costs with bank credit. USD.AI already publishes model, count and stated value for every pledge. Borrowers could make the asset side easier to underwrite by providing independently attestable utilization, contractual revenues, power expense, location, title and recovery rights. Whether such disclosure would reduce borrowing costs relative to the observed \RateWavg{} weighted-average coupon remains an empirical question.

\emph{Researchers.} The dataset is frozen and hash-verified, every number reproduces offline, and the method transfers to any GPU-credit protocol on an indexed chain. The open questions are the ones the chain cannot answer: independent collateral valuations, borrower revenue, and recoveries once the first loan defaults. A second protocol and a longer window would make this a comparative study.

\subsection{Monitoring indicators}
The pipeline can run on a schedule. We suggest six indicators, each computed by code in the repository:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Cash coverage} $\kappa$ by month, split into borrower interest and base yield.
\item \emph{Pre-funded share}: \texttt{depositTimelockBalance} over \texttt{totalAssets}, with the age of each open escrow.
\item \emph{Borrower HHI} on outstanding principal, and the largest borrower's share.
\item \emph{Queue pressure}: pending redemptions against idle deposit balance, and the age of the oldest pending request.
\item \emph{Missed payments}: loans whose last repayment is older than their repayment interval.
\item \emph{Peg stress}: the market discount of USDai and sUSDai to NAV.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Other GPU-backed protocols}
Other protocols also tokenize GPU financing or GPU revenue, and some issue their own synthetic dollars, such as GAIB's AID~\cite{gaib2025}. The same method works for any of them whose contracts run on a chain GoldRush indexes: decode from source, rebuild the loan book, reconcile against state and trace the cash. Comparing them on cash coverage, pre-funding and borrower concentration is a natural next study.

\subsection{Data coverage}
Without a unified API, several steps would have needed an archive node and custom indexing. GoldRush provided complete decoded histories for the lending contracts, event scans across 182 block windows, holder lists on four chains, holder lists at past blocks, one call listing every chain an address has used, cross-chain wallet balances and daily token prices. Three gaps remain. GoldRush does not offer \texttt{eth\_call}, so the state check used a public RPC. Holder lists at past blocks are available only on its Foundational chains, so we have them for Ethereum but not Arbitrum. Akash and other Cosmos chains are not indexed.

\subsection{Limitations}
We study one protocol over 17 months. The loan book is reconstructed from selected events, and both the API and the node checks use those same event definitions; identical raw logs do not independently prove complete contract coverage, correct borrower identities or the economic meaning of every transaction. The state check is at the same block as the event study, but the holder snapshots are at a nearby block and the USDai reserve observation uses a later one. Two topic hashes on the V1 router match no signature in the source history. Hardware-token metadata is mutable and was retrieved after the study cutoff; the aggregate GPU count applies a server-model assumption and excludes mixed-unit rows. Holder-address concentration is not beneficial-owner concentration, and current holder lists give it at a single point in time. Contract labels come from GoldRush metadata and can be wrong. Completed redemption waits exclude still-pending requests, and most loans have not matured. The H100 price series is neither model-matched to the B200/B300-heavy collateral nor a record of borrower realized revenue. Livepeer payments mix video transcoding and AI inference, and the Akash figures come from the network's own API. We have not independently verified executed loan documents, title, collateral custody, enforceable insurance, leases, utilization, operating expenses or customer receivables. No off-chain person or entity is attributed to a wallet, and the data do not demonstrate misuse of funds.

\subsection{Ethics and right of reply}
All data is public. We do not name individuals or link wallets to companies. At the time of writing, the escrow findings in Section~\ref{sec:escrow} have not yet been put to USD.AI. We will share them with the protocol before publication and include any response in a revised version; until then, the reading of those findings is ours alone.

\section{Conclusion}
Event logs allow a detailed reconstruction of recorded GPU-backed lending and vault flows: \Originated{} in gross loan originations to \NBorrowers{} borrower addresses, historical income from borrower payments and base yield, lumpy redemption servicing, and \DTShare{} of reported assets attributed to pre-funded escrows without matching on-chain loan originations at the snapshot. These are financial-observability findings, not evidence that credit has outrun underlying compute demand. Their significance is the separation between auditable financial records and off-chain collateral, contracted revenue and legal recovery. A credible machine-credit risk measure will require both sets of information. Comparing this framework across protocols and testing whether its indicators forecast repayment, liquidity or recovery outcomes remain tasks for further research.
