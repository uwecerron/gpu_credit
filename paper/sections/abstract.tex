\begin{abstract}
This study develops and applies an event-based reconstruction of GPU-backed private credit, reconciling lending records with vault accounting and tracing the boundary between observable financial claims and unverifiable off-chain collateral and operating cash flows. Several synthetic-dollar protocols take stablecoin deposits and lend them to companies that operate GPU clusters. The hardware, the borrowers' revenues and the loan agreements are off-chain, while loan and vault transactions are recorded on a public chain. We reconstruct USD.AI on Arbitrum from \EventsDecoded{} decoded events between \StudyStart{} and \StudyEnd{}, using the Covalent GoldRush API and event signatures from the protocol's source code. We identify \NLoans{} loans to \NBorrowers{} borrower addresses, with \Originated{} originated and \Outstanding{} outstanding at the study cutoff. No breach or liquidation event was recorded; most active loans have not reached maturity. Protocol-hosted metadata describes about \CollGPUs{} pledged GPUs at \BookLTV{} stated loan-to-value, but does not independently establish custody or realizable collateral value. Annualized deposit-side NAV growth was \AnnTotal{}. An event-based cash-coverage measure was \CashCoverage{}, with historical base yield from stablecoin reserves and incentives exceeding borrower interest; counting borrower payments alone, it was \KappaBorrowers{}. Completed redemption requests had a median wait of \WaitMedian{} days; pending requests remain censored. A same-block accounting check at block \StateBlock{} puts the reconstructed loan book within \LoanBookGapPct{} of the vault's reported balance and the share count within \SharesGapPct{}. At that snapshot, \DTShare{} of reported assets was attributed to two pre-funded escrows without matching on-chain originations; transfers to external wallets do not establish the legal status or recoverability of those claims. We also measure \ComputeSpend{} in payments on Akash and Livepeer. Comparing those payments with \Originated{} of loan originations is descriptive, not a like-for-like measure of compute demand. The accompanying code and frozen dataset document the reconstruction; operational cash flows and legal collateral rights require separate verification.
\end{abstract}
